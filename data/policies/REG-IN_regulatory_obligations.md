# REG-IN — Summary of Indian AML Reporting Obligations

*Plain-language summary prepared for the MuleTrace demo. It paraphrases public rules for context only and is not legal advice; always check the current text of the Act, Rules and RBI Master Directions.*

## §1 Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA)
PMLA makes money laundering a criminal offence and places obligations on "reporting entities", which include banks and NBFCs. Reporting entities must verify customer identity, maintain records of transactions, and furnish information on prescribed transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit – India (FIU-IND).

## §2 Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR)
Under the PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, a reporting entity must report suspicious transactions, whether cash or non-cash, including a series of integrally connected transactions. The STR must be furnished to the Director, FIU-IND, within seven working days of the Principal Officer arriving at a conclusion that the transactions are suspicious. Attempted transactions must also be reported.

## §3 Cash Transaction Reports (CTR)
Cash transactions of more than ₹10 lakh, or a series of integrally connected cash transactions below ₹10 lakh each that together exceed ₹10 lakh within a month, must be reported to FIU-IND by the 15th of the following month.

## §4 Principal Officer and Designated Director
Each reporting entity must appoint a Principal Officer responsible for furnishing information to FIU-IND, and a Designated Director responsible for overall compliance. The Principal Officer must record the reasons for treating transactions as suspicious.

## §5 RBI Master Direction on KYC
Regulated entities must follow a risk-based approach: categorise customers by risk, carry out ongoing due diligence, monitor transactions for consistency with the customer's profile, and apply enhanced monitoring to high-risk accounts. Unusual patterns, such as transactions inconsistent with declared income or business, must be examined.

## §6 Record retention
Records of transactions and of customer identification must be kept for at least five years after the transaction or the end of the business relationship, in a form that allows transactions to be reconstructed as evidence.

## §7 Confidentiality and tipping-off
Reporting entities and their staff must keep the filing of an STR confidential and must not tip off the customer.

## §8 Mule-account initiatives
Mule accounts are a national priority: the Ministry of Home Affairs reported in February 2025 that over 19 lakh mule accounts had been flagged, and the Reserve Bank Innovation Hub has developed MuleHunter.AI to help banks identify mule accounts.
